No EPF in Germany? You can still get money from your employer for your pension. 🇮🇳➡️🇩🇪

Every employee in Germany has a legal right to a company pension (bAV, "Entgeltumwandlung"): part of your gross salary goes straight into a pension plan.

And here's what most people miss: if your employer saves social security this way, they must add 15% on top (§1a BetrAVG).

💶 Example: you put in €200 a month → your employer adds up to €30 → €2,760 a year instead of €2,400.

Why this matters for expats: you started working here later, so you have fewer years to build your German pension. Every euro your employer adds counts.

👉 The question for HR: "Do we offer Entgeltumwandlung with the employer subsidy (Arbeitgeberzuschuss) under §1a BetrAVG?"

Collective agreements can have different rules, and for salaries above the contribution limits the subsidy can be lower.
