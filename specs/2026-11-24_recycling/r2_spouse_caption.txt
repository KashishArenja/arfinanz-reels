Germany passed it in July 2026 (GKV-Beitragssatzstabilisierungsgesetz) – and most expat families still haven't heard about it.

1️⃣ The spouse surcharge (from 1 January 2028)
Today your husband or wife without income is insured with you for €0 (family insurance). From 2028 you pay an extra 2.5% of your income for them – unless one of these applies:
• a child under 12 lives with you
• a child with a disability
• your partner cares for a relative
• your partner has reached pension age (plus a few exceptions for illness/disability)

Example: €5,000 a month before tax → about €125 a month, €1,500 a year. Your employer pays nothing of it. Income above the contribution ceiling isn't counted.

Your kids stay insured for free.

2️⃣ Smaller changes from 2027
• Co-payments for medicine €7.50–€15 instead of €5–€10
• Less money from the insurance for dentures (–10 percentage points)
• Contribution ceiling and the limit for private insurance go up by an extra €300 a month

3️⃣ Why it hits expat families hardest
Often one partner works and the other is on a spouse visa, learning German or waiting for their degree to be recognised. That's exactly the household that pays now.

You have until 2028 to plan: a job for your partner above mini-job level, self-employment, or comparing public vs. private insurance for the whole family. What's right depends on your numbers and your visa.
